What Is Wedding Insurance?

Wedding insurance is a specialized policy that protects couples from financial losses caused by unexpected events before or during their wedding. Think of it as a safety net for your deposits and payments. Typically, coverage falls into two categories: cancellation/postponement insurance and liability insurance. Many policies combine both in a package.

With the average US wedding costing over $30,000, wedding insurance provides essential financial protection. Policies can reimburse you for non-refundable expenses if you must cancel or postpone due to covered reasons like severe weather, vendor failure, illness, or venue issues. Liability coverage protects you if a guest is injured or property is damaged at the wedding.

Pro Tip: Before buying a policy, check whether your venue or vendors already have liability coverage – you may be able to reduce your own policy costs.

Types of Wedding Insurance Coverage

Cancellation/Postponement Insurance – This covers you if you need to cancel or postpone your wedding for reasons beyond your control. Covered events often include sudden illness or injury of the bride, groom, or immediate family; death of a key participant; severe weather; venue damage; or vendor bankruptcy or no-show. You’ll receive reimbursement for lost deposits and non-refundable payments up to the policy limit.

Liability Insurance – This covers legal and medical costs if a guest is injured during the wedding or reception, or if you accidentally damage the venue. Many venues require couples to carry at least $1 million in liability coverage. Some policies also include liquor liability if alcohol is served.

Additional Coverage Options – You can often add coverage for specific items like wedding attire, rings, gifts, photography equipment, and even honeymoon trip cancellation. Some insurers offer 'vendor failure' endorsements that protect you if a vendor goes out of business.

Common Perils Covered

While policies vary, most wedding insurance plans cover the following common perils:

  • Illness or injury – The bride, groom, or key family member becomes too ill to attend or the wedding is canceled due to hospitalization.
  • Severe weather – Hurricane, snowstorm, flood, or other events that make travel to the venue impossible or dangerous.
  • Vendor no-show or bankruptcy – A key vendor like the caterer or photographer fails to show up or goes out of business.
  • Venue damage – Fire, vandalism, or structural issues force the venue to close.
  • Military deployment – If the bride, groom, or immediate family member is unexpectedly deployed.
  • Jury duty – If a key participant is summoned for jury duty on the wedding day.

Some policies also cover lost or damaged wedding attire, rings, or gifts.

What Wedding Insurance Doesn't Cover

It’s equally important to know what's excluded. Most wedding policies do not cover:

  • Cold feet or change of heart – If you simply decide not to get married.
  • Pre-existing conditions – Illnesses that existed before you bought the policy.
  • Unforeseeable events – Many policies exclude terrorism, war, nuclear hazards, and pandemics unless specifically added.
  • Acts of negligence – If you fail to take reasonable precautions, like not having a backup plan for outdoor weather.
  • Financial problems or budget cuts – If you run out of money or decide to downgrade.
  • Normal weather conditions – Unless there’s a severe storm warning.

Read your policy carefully so you know exactly what risks are covered.

How Much Does Wedding Insurance Cost?

Wedding insurance is relatively affordable compared to the overall cost of a wedding. A basic cancellation/postponement policy starts around $100 to $200 for coverage of $5,000 to $10,000 in expenses. Comprehensive policies that include liability and add-ons can cost $400 to $800 for $1 million in liability coverage. The price depends on your total wedding budget, location, number of guests, and desired limits.

For example, a policy covering a $30,000 wedding with $1 million liability might cost between $450 and $600. You can often get a discount if you buy the policy online or bundle with other insurance. It’s wise to get quotes from multiple providers to compare coverage and cost.

How to Choose a Wedding Insurance Policy

Follow these steps to select the right policy:

  1. Assess your risks – Consider your venue (outdoor vs. indoor), guest count, travel distance, vendor contracts, and any special elements like fireworks or rented equipment.
  2. Check venue requirements – Many venues mandate liability coverage. Ask what minimum limits you need.
  3. Compare policies – Get quotes from companies like WedSafe, Travelers, Markel, and AIG. Use CoverageFixPro to compare insurance options side by side.
  4. Review exclusions – Know what isn’t covered, especially for weather or vendor issues.
  5. Consider add-ons – Add coverage for rings, gifts, and honeymoon if needed.

Remember that you can usually customize the policy limits to match your budget.

When to Buy Wedding Insurance

Buy wedding insurance as soon as you start making deposits – ideally 12 to 18 months before your wedding date. Most policies must be purchased at least one year in advance to cover pre-existing conditions. If you wait until later, you may lose coverage for certain events. Also, some policies have a 'time-sensitive' clause that requires you to buy within 14 days of your first deposit to cover vendor defaults. Don’t wait until the last minute; securing insurance early protects your investment from day one.

Real-Life Scenarios Where Wedding Insurance Saved the Day

Scenario 1: A bride broke her leg two weeks before the wedding. The postponement coverage reimbursed the couple for non-refundable deposits and helped them reschedule without financial loss.

Scenario 2: A photographer went out of business three days before the wedding. The policy covered the cost of hiring a new photographer on short notice.

Scenario 3: A hurricane forced the cancellation of an outdoor beach wedding. The couple received a full refund of deposits for the venue, catering, and flowers.

Scenario 4: A guest slipped on a wet floor at the reception and sued the couple. The liability coverage paid for legal fees and medical expenses up to the policy limit.
